## Page summary
This is the landing page of the "Testing Reality" site. It is not a lesson; it is a hook plus a table of contents. A hero banner announces "Quantum Experiments: From simple polarizers to the double-slit quantum eraser: A pedagogical journey through the heart of quantum mystery." A section called "The mystery" lists the three pop-science claims the site intends to test against the formalism and against real optics experiments: superposition / wave-function collapse ("as if the Moon were only there when someone looked at it"), entanglement as a faster-than-light influence, and the Many-Worlds multiverse. Each claim ends with a sceptical question in bold ("But is that really how it works?", "Is this just spooky storytelling, or does quantum theory truly defy relativity?", "But what, if anything, does Many-Worlds add to the equations?"). A short call-to-action promises to walk the reader through the mathematics and through "detailed and clear experiments", some hands-on via interactive simulators. The page ends with the "Experiments" index: eight cards (Introduction + Experiments 1-7), each with a schematic thumbnail of the optical setup, which is the canonical left-to-right layout the game's 3D lab should reproduce.

### [p.5-7] Experiments index grid
Eight cards in a two-column grid. Each card = a pale blue-grey rounded thumbnail panel (~1.77:1), a small purple-blue uppercase kicker ("INTRODUCTION", "EXPERIMENT 1", ...), and a bold black serif title. Each is a link to the corresponding page. The thumbnails share one visual language, which the game should adopt as its schematic style:

Common schematic vocabulary (flat vector, pastel):
- **LASER**: salmon/pink filled rectangle with a small rectangular output nub on its right side; pink pill label "LASER" below.
- **Unlabelled grey hatched disc** immediately after the laser in every setup (diagonal hatching, grey outline). The site never names it on this page; in the experiment pages it is the beam-conditioning element (assumed: a neutral-density attenuator / lens; see Open questions).
- **Polarizer**: a circle filled with hatching whose direction encodes the transmission axis: vertical hatch = "90°", horizontal hatch = "0°", diagonal hatch = "45°". Purple fill = the polarizer whose angle is a free variable in that experiment (sometimes with a curved black arrow above it meaning "rotatable"); grey fill = fixed polarizer. Pill label below gives the name (P1, P2, P3, PA, PB).
- **Slit barrier**: a thick black vertical bar broken by one gap (single slit) or two gaps (double slit).
- **BB**: two thin light-blue vertical rectangles side by side with a blue pill label "BB" (pair of BBO down-conversion crystals, the entangled-photon-pair source).
- **Mirror**: a short, thick black bar tilted ~30° from horizontal.
- **Detector D1 / D2**: tall salmon rectangle with pink pill label; when two detectors exist they are joined by thin black wires to a small rounded black-outlined pill labelled "coin" (coincidence counter) standing vertically to the right.
- **Beam**: thin red-orange line; solid for classical-intensity experiments (Exp 1-3), dashed for the single-photon / photon-pair experiments (Exp 4-7).
- **State planes**: dashed vertical grey lines with bold grey labels Ψ0, Ψ1, Ψ2, ... beneath, marking where the quantum state is evaluated along the beam (Ψ0 just after the laser, next one after each element, last one at the detector). These labels are the hooks to the equations on each experiment page.

#### [p.5] Card: EXPERIMENT 1 — "Polarizers"
- Figure: four stacked beam lines (rows), each starting with LASER + grey disc and ending at D1, state planes Ψ0, Ψ1, Ψ2, Ψ3 shared by all rows:
  1. Row 1: LASER → grey disc → D1 (no polarizer).
  2. Row 2: LASER → grey disc → P1 (purple, "90°", vertical hatch) → D1.
  3. Row 3: LASER → grey disc → P1 ("90°") → P2 (purple, "0°", horizontal hatch) → D1.
  4. Row 4: LASER → grey disc → P1 ("90°") → P2 (purple, "45", diagonal hatch) → P3 (purple, "0°") → D1.
  P1 sits at Ψ1, P2 (row 4's 45°) between Ψ1 and Ψ2, the 0° polarizer at Ψ2, D1 at Ψ3. This is the classic "crossed polarizers go dark, inserting a 45° one in between lets light through" demonstration.

#### [p.5] Card: EXPERIMENT 2 — "Single and double slit"
- Figure: two rows. Row 1: LASER → grey disc → single-slit barrier → straight beam → D1. Row 2: LASER → grey disc → the beam fans into two lines that pass through the two gaps of a double-slit barrier and re-converge on D1 (diamond-shaped path). Planes: Ψ0 after laser, Ψ1 and Ψ2 drawn adjacent at the slit plane (just before / just after), Ψ3 at the detector.

#### [p.5] Card: EXPERIMENT 3 — "Polarized double slit"
- Figure: single row. LASER → grey disc → beam fans to the double slit; immediately behind the upper slit a grey polarizer "PA" at "0°" (horizontal hatch), behind the lower slit a grey polarizer "PB" at "90°" (vertical hatch); the two paths converge through a purple polarizer "P1" at "45°" (diagonal hatch, curved rotation arrow above it) → D1. Planes Ψ0, Ψ1 Ψ2 (slit + PA/PB), Ψ3 (detector). Which-path marking by orthogonal polarizers, then erasure by the 45° analyser.

#### [p.6] Card: EXPERIMENT 4 — "Entanglement and Bell state"
- Figure: LASER → grey disc → BB (blue pair of crystals) at Ψ1. From BB two dashed red beams leave: the upper one runs diagonally up-right to a tilted black mirror and is reflected horizontally to the right, passing a purple polarizer labelled "α" (pill "P2", rotation arrow) into D2; the lower one runs straight right through a purple polarizer labelled "β" (pill "P1", rotation arrow) into D1. D1 and D2 are wired to the "coin" pill. Planes: Ψ0, Ψ1 (after BB), Ψ2 (at the polarizer/detector column). The polarizer labels are Greek letters (α, β) rather than numbers: the angles are the free variables of a Bell-type measurement.

#### [p.6] Card: EXPERIMENT 5 — "Puting it all together" (sic, site typo)
- Figure: LASER → grey disc → BB. Upper (idler) beam: mirror → straight to D2 with no polarizer. Lower (signal) beam: fans into two dashed paths through a double slit with grey PA "0°" (upper) and PB "90°" (lower) behind the slits, converging through grey polarizer P1 "45°" (no rotation arrow) into D1. D1, D2 → coin. Planes Ψ0, Ψ1 (after BB), Ψ2 (slits), Ψ3 (after PA/PB), Ψ4 (P1), Ψ5 (after P1), Ψ6 (detectors).

#### [p.7] Card: EXPERIMENT 6 — "Delayed choice quantum eraser"
- Figure: identical to Experiment 5 except the upper (idler) arm now carries a purple polarizer "P2" at "45°" (diagonal hatch, rotation arrow) before D2. Planes Ψ0, Ψ1, Ψ2, Ψ3, Ψ4, Ψ5-6 (combined label), Ψ7.

#### [p.7] Card: "EXPERIEMENT 7" (sic, site typo) — "Detecting, but not recording"
- Figure: identical to Experiment 6 except P2 on the idler arm is a purple polarizer at "90°" (vertical hatch, no rotation arrow) and is followed by a thick black vertical bar (a beam block / shutter) standing in the beam before D2, i.e. the idler is "detected" (interacts) but never "recorded". Planes Ψ0, Ψ1, Ψ2, Ψ3, Ψ4, Ψ5-6, Ψ7. The "coin" link is still drawn.

## Physics model for simulation
The Home page itself states no equations or numbers. What it fixes is the catalogue of configurations, which the simulation must support (details and exact probabilities are on each experiment page, files 01-09):

| # | Setup (from thumbnail) | State type the page will need | Free parameters shown |
|---|---|---|---|
| Intro | none | single qubit, real amplitudes (2-D vectors) | none |
| 1 | laser → P1(90°) → [P2(45°)] → P3(0°) → D1 | polarization qubit; Malus's law cos² | P1, P2, P3 angles (purple) |
| 2 | laser → single / double slit → D1 | path qubit (two slits) + screen position (interference) | slit count |
| 3 | double slit with PA 0°, PB 90° → P1 45° → D1 | path ⊗ polarization (2×2); which-path marking and erasure | P1 angle (rotatable) |
| 4 | laser → BB → mirror / direct → P2(α), P1(β) → D2, D1 → coin | two-photon polarization Bell state; coincidence rate vs α−β | α, β |
| 5 | BB; idler → D2; signal → PA/PB slits → P1 45° → D1; coin | Bell state ⊗ path; no interference in coincidence | none (all grey) |
| 6 | as 5 plus P2(45°) rotatable on idler → D2 | delayed-choice erasure; fringes restored in coincidence | P2 angle |
| 7 | as 6 with P2 = 90° and a beam block before D2 | measurement without recording | none |

Common elements of the model (stated on this page only through the diagrams): the quantum state is tracked plane-by-plane (Ψ0 → Ψ1 → ...), each optical element is an operator between planes, and detectors give probabilities. Wavelength, slit dimensions, distances and count rates are not given on the Home page (see experiment pages; the project memory notes a 405 nm violet diode laser for the real lab).

## Teaching notes
Ideas the Home page assumes (none are taught here; they are set up as questions):
1. The pop-science vocabulary: superposition, wave-function collapse, observation, randomness ("roll of the dice"), entanglement, "spooky" faster-than-light influence, relativity's speed limit, Many-Worlds / multiverse, Schrödinger's cat.
2. The site's pedagogical stance, which the game should mirror: each myth is stated in its popular form, then met with a sceptical bold question; the promise is to check claims against (a) the mathematical formalism and (b) experimental observation; progression goes from simple (polarizers) to complex (delayed-choice eraser).
3. The diagram language (laser → elements → detector, with Ψn planes) is introduced visually here before any equation; the game can teach "state planes" as checkpoints along the beam.
4. Order of the curriculum: Introduction (2-D vectors) → Exp 1 (Malus's law) → Exp 2 (interference) → Exp 3 (which-path + eraser) → Exp 4 (entanglement/Bell) → Exp 5 (entanglement + slits) → Exp 6 (delayed choice) → Exp 7 (detecting without recording).
